Each note has seven parts: the question, the central claim, the evidence, the key findings, the limits, the theory, and the earlier work the paper argues with. Every claim must be backed by a word-for-word quote with its page number. The accepted answers come from each paper's published peer review history and were checked by a human labeler. Every note on this page was matched to those answers by the same judge, a set of Claude Opus 5.5 agents working from the labeler's written rules.

Coverage: how many accepted answers each model found

Coverage is the share of accepted answers a note states with at least one word-for-word quote behind it. A half point goes to an answer that is incomplete or placed in the wrong part of the note. An answer stated across two entries of the same part counts in full. Bars show the mean of a model's runs, and the black line its lowest and highest run.

Scorecard

Core findings are the results at least two peer reviewers restated. A risk flag is a claim that says more than the paper does. A contradiction is a place where the paper disagrees with itself, such as an abstract that claims more than the results show. Mild flags are small overstatements, such as "revise" where the paper says "challenge"; serious flags are wrong directions, invented claims and wrong attributions. Each model reached through an API was run three times; quote counts are totals over its runs, and the local model has one run because it runs at temperature 0.

What a harness adds

A harness is code around the model that checks and extends its note without ever seeing the accepted answers. It can confirm every quote against the paper and send failures back for repair, ask the model to re-check each claim against its quoted passage, and ask the model what else the paper states. Each row is the same model with a different setting, starting from the note of its first run.

Confirming quotes helps in proportion to how many quotes a model gets wrong. Re-checking removes errors from a model that makes many and removes correct claims from a model that makes few. Asking for more helps models that are accurate but stop early.

How to read these numbers, and what they do not show yet

Each API model was run three times on each paper. Across runs no model moved more than four points, so the three groups of models are well apart, but differences of a few points within a group mean nothing. Five papers is a small set, all from one journal and all in social science; a single model's score on a single paper moves by up to eight points between runs.

The human labeler independently labeled 60 accepted answers, weighted toward hard cases. The agents gave the same label on 52 of them (87%, Cohen's kappa 0.79). Where they differed, the agents were usually the stricter.

Whether a quote appears in the paper, and on the stated page, is checked by code. Which accepted answer a claim matches was judged by Claude Opus 5.5 agents, one per paper, each working from the gold answers, the paper and the labeler's written rules. On the notes that were also matched by hand, the agents gave the same credit on 87% to 90% of accepted answers (Cohen's kappa 0.68 to 0.83). The human labeler has not yet reviewed the matching.

In the harness table, a setting that only drops claims or repairs quotes carries its matches over from the note it started from; whether each repaired quote supports its claim was not judged separately.

The accepted answers were drafted from each paper's published reviews and author responses, then corrected by one human labeler. Answers that a model suggested are never added to the list, so a model cannot earn credit for points only it raised.

The local model sometimes wrote a reply whose brackets did not balance. Such a reply is read one part at a time, so a formatting slip does not cost a model its whole note.
